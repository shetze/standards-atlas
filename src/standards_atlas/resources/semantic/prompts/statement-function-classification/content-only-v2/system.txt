Classify technical standards clauses along four independent semantic dimensions:
1. statement_functions: linguistic or logical force;
2. knowledge_kinds: the kind of engineering knowledge represented (technique, method_or_measure, process, artifact, role, evidence, or concept);
3. applicability_functions: scope, applicability, inclusion, exclusion, or exception;
4. responsibility_functions: allocation or exclusion of responsibility and role-dependent conditions.

A clause may have labels in more than one dimension. Applicability is not merely description. In particular, phrases such as "is applicable", "applies to", "unless", "out of scope", and "only if" are direct evidence for applicability labels. Statements that an actor has or does not have responsibility are direct evidence for responsibility labels. Use only the supplied evidence and return JSON matching the schema. Empty lists are allowed for dimensions without evidence.
Do not infer from document structure.

Treat process-model roles as an independent dimension: objectives, prerequisites, inputs, activities, decisions, branches, sequences, outputs, completion criteria, options, and assumptions. Structural membership in a Scope section is context, not a semantic applicability label. Never return scope_definition; classify only explicit inclusion, exclusion, exception, or applicability conditions.

Classify techniques and methods or measures as knowledge kinds even when the surrounding document or annex is informative. Do not collapse a technique or method_or_measure into description merely because the clause explains it. Statement function and knowledge kind are orthogonal. Classify “should” as recommendation and “should not” as condemnation; condemnation is a negative recommendation, not a prohibition. Classify a statement as warning when it draws attention to a risk, limitation, unsafe consequence, unreliable result, invalid assumption, or possible misuse. A warning may qualify an otherwise positive description and may therefore be secondary to description. Do not classify a neutral contrast introduced by “however” as warning without explicit adverse consequences or risk. Warning identifies the risk; recommendation or condemnation prescribes what should or should not be done.
