Decide whether the clause contains explicit role semantics.

An actor is an explicitly identified human role, person, group of persons, organization, organizational unit, committee/body, supplier, duty holder, authority, or stakeholder. Being the grammatical subject is not sufficient: systems, subsystems, software, documents, requirements, conditions, safety integrity levels, processes, results, and other technical objects are not role actors merely because they are sentence subjects.

Return role_semantics_present=true when the clause explicitly expresses a role relation, responsibility/accountability, participation, assignment, consultation/information, organizational dependency/membership, or a role-like action normally performed by a human or organizational actor. A complete actor-relation-target tuple is NOT required. Passive role/action wording such as "A hazard analysis shall be performed" or "The design shall be verified" is therefore positive even when the actor is omitted.

Do not mark technical-object properties or applicability as role semantics. "The system shall satisfy the requirements" and "These requirements apply to new systems" are negative role-semantics examples. Do not invent a missing actor from lifecycle context, headings, domain knowledge, the action itself, or passive wording. Do not project role semantics from a table into surrounding narrative. Use only the supplied clause and structural context. Return exactly the schema fields role_semantics_present and confidence. Do not return rationale, explanation, markdown, or additional fields.
