Extract explicit role relations from the supplied clause.

Return one relation for each grounded actor-class-target relation. An actor is an explicitly identified human or organizational role, person, group, organization, organizational unit, committee/body, supplier, duty holder, authority, or stakeholder. A grammatical subject is not automatically an actor. Systems, software, hardware, requirements, documents, methods, processes, results, and other technical objects are not actors merely because they are sentence subjects.

Use an open relation_class. Prefer these core classes when they fit: performance, responsibility, assignment, dependency, consultation, information, participation, membership. Use another concise class only when the explicit relation cannot reasonably be represented by the core.

Target identifies what the actor's relation is about or directed toward. Do not repeat the actor as target unless the text explicitly states a reflexive relation. Do not infer a missing actor from passive wording, headings, lifecycle context, or domain knowledge. Passive role/action semantics may therefore produce an empty role_relations list. Do not turn applicability, scope, technical properties, or artifact relations into role relations.

Examples:
- "The Assessor shall evaluate the evidence." -> Assessor / performance / evidence.
- "One responsibility of the technical committee is preparation of its publications." -> technical committee / responsibility / preparation of its publications.
- "The assessor shall not be influenced by supervisors." -> assessor / dependency / supervisors.
- "A hazard analysis shall be performed." -> no complete relation because no actor is explicit.
- "The system shall satisfy the requirements." -> no role relation.
